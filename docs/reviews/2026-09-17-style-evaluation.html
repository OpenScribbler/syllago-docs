A promising architecture, but Jev has not earned a broad rollout yet.

Vale efficiently shrank a deterministic backlog. Jev performed well on a small synthetic fixture set, then surfaced no candidates on three real pages while consuming 44,347 tokens. The right next move is a larger, author-labeled real-doc corpus—not automatic rewriting.

Decision: continue as a bounded research spike. The queue-and-patch workflow is useful. The present semantic rules need better real-world recall evidence and a cheaper request shape before they belong in CI.

A safe, deterministic Vale fix.

Review

Split one dense procedure paragraph

A human inspection of a Jev near miss suggests separating interaction steps from the resulting config and optional shortcut. This is useful editorial work, but the calibrated queue failed to surface it.

Reject

Do not rewrite product names containing “CLI”

Copilot CLI is a name, and CLI is accepted audience vocabulary. Add a targeted vocabulary exception; do not accept Google.WordList blindly.

What this says about value

Vale should remain the first pass. It is fast, transparent, and excellent for mechanical rules. Jev is most promising as a second-pass classifier for a small set of carefully defined semantic questions, with evidence and confidence attached—not as a style-guide ingest or autonomous rewriter.

Before investing further, label roughly 20–30 naturally occurring paragraphs per rule, include hard near-boundary cases, and measure recall at a fixed precision target. Also reduce payload cost by sending only eligible units per rule or testing a two-stage candidate filter. If Jev cannot surface useful real findings at tolerable cost, stop; the combined queue still remains useful with Vale alone.
